A higher-level communication starts when the next, higher `group` value is assigned. Group values are integers, unique per communication, and increase by 1 each time a new communication starts. They are used to differentiate between separate higher-level communications. An element in a higher-level communication can either be `<user_input>` (representing user keyboard presses or cut-and-paste buffer pastes) or `<system_output>` (representing responses from software). Each element includes a `timestamp` (in seconds) that indicates how much time has passed since the session began. Elements that are part of the same higher-level communication will each have the same `group`. When a higher-level communication is complete, it is followed by the first element of the next higher-level communication, with the next `group`.

The `User` section ends with two elements. The former of the two is the last part of the prior higher-level communication (with a `group`), and the latter element is the one you will evaluate (with no `group` assigned).

Your task is to determine whether the final ungrouped element belongs to the same higher-level communication as the one before it, or starts a new one. The final higher-level communication in the `User` section may or may not be complete; determining this may be required to complete your task.
