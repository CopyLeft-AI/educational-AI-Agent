# Goal
Your goal is to use a set of higher-level-communications (HLCs) and one final possibly incomplete HLC to determine whether the final event you are given is a part of the final HLC, or begins a new one.

# Definitions
A higher-level communication (HLC) is a series of related events, representing a single idea, concept, or datum.
 * The first HLC starts at the beginning of the data you are evaluating.
 * Each HLC in the data (excepting the first HLC) starts where the previous HLC stops, and is assigned the next, higher `group` identifier.
 * Each HLC in the data (excepting the final HLC) is completed when it is followed by the first event of the next HLC, with the next `group`.
 * The final HLC may or may not be complete; determining this may be required to complete your task.
 * HLCs may end with either an `user_input` event, or `system_output`.

Examples of HLCs include:
 * A command
 * A response to a command
 * A system message
 * A complete keyboard shortcut
 * An escape sequence that redraws a portion of the screen
 * A series of backspaces, deletions, navigations, or additions causing a typo
 * A series of backspaces, deletions, navigations, or additions correcting a typo

An event captures communication in a text terminal session.
 * Events can either be `<user_input>` (representing user keyboard presses or cut-and-paste buffer pastes) or `<system_output>` (representing responses from software).
 * All events include a `timestamp` (in seconds) that indicates how much time has passed since the session began.
 * Events are always provided in chronological order; older events will come before newer events in the data you are evaluating.
 * Events that are part of the same HLC should each have the same `group`, with the exception of the final HLC, which may be missing events.
 * Events with a `group` of `X` have not had a valid group assigned, and may be a part of the final HLC, may start a new HLC, or may end the final HLC.

Each `group` is identified by an integer.
 * They are used to identify a complete HLC, are unique, and increase by 1 each time one HLC stops, and another starts.
 * They can be used to identify events belonging to an HLC.

# Instructions:
You will be given data to be evaluated within a pair of `data` tags which will contain a series of terminal session events, with all but the final event having a valid `group`, and all but the final HLC being known to be complete.
At the end, you will find a final (possibly incomplete) HLC, followed by an event with an invalid `group` of `X`, and with the highest timestamp. This final event (with a `group` of `X`) is the event you must classify.
Your task is to determine whether the final event is part of the final HLC, or if the event starts a new HLC.

## How to Respond:

Respond with the following two items:
 * An explanation in English less than or equal to 200 characters in length on why you believe the final event is part of the final HLC, or why it is not. Do not add code blocks, or other multi-line formatting.
 * An answer, either the integer `group` of the final HLC, or`NEW`

Use the following template to format your response:
```xml
<explanation/>
Answer: <answer/>
```
